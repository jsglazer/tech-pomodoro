A menu-bar-only Pomodoro timer for macOS, with a companion iPhone and iPad app, built around a multi-tier schedule: work/rest reps build a cycle, cycles build a session, and each level gets its own break.

## Why

Most Pomodoro apps stop at "work, rest, long break". tech-pomodoro adds the level above that — a session of cycles with its own long rest at the end, optionally repeating forever — and puts the countdown itself in the menu bar, colour-shifting as each phase runs out.

## Features

- **Multi-tier schedule.** Work and rest alternate for the reps of a cycle; the cycle closes with a long break; a session of cycles closes with a session rest. Every duration and count is configurable — type a number directly or use the stepper — and the session can repeat indefinitely.
- **The countdown is the control.** Click the numbers in the popover to pause or resume; Start (which becomes Stop once running), Pause and Skip sit beneath them. Pause is filled yellow and Resume filled green, so the button shows which way it goes. Stop asks for confirmation first, since it resets the schedule to Rep 1 of Cycle 1. Skip ends the current phase at once and starts the next one running, even from a pause; the reps and cycles advance as if the phase had finished, a skipped Work interval is logged as the time actually worked, and no alert fires.
- **Menu bar as a countdown.** Show minutes remaining or a clock icon, optionally on a filled background. A phase letter follows the time — `24W` during Work, `4R` during Rest, Long Break or Session Rest — and sits beside the icon in clock-icon mode. In every phase the minutes turn yellow at a warning threshold and red at an alert threshold — both configurable by typing or stepping, either one disabled by setting it to 0. Above the thresholds the rest phases get their own colour (green by default), and the ordinary countdown takes a colour of your choosing. A paused timer shows in red in either mode. When the timer is idle, the clock icon shows in cyan so the app stays easy to spot.
- **Alerts.** A system-sound ding and a menu bar flash at every interval, cycle, and session boundary — each independently switchable, and each repeatable up to ten times. Sounds are read from this Mac's sound folders, so the picker never offers something that will not play. An optional modal dialog can also appear at every boundary, for when a sound or a flash is easy to miss; it closes itself after a set time (15 seconds by default) or, set to never, waits for a click.
- **Sleep-aware.** By default (**Resume**) a running timer freezes when the Mac sleeps and picks up on wake exactly where it left off, with the same phase and the same time remaining and no click needed. A pause you made yourself stays paused. **Keep counting** instead runs against the wall clock through the sleep and fast-forwards on wake to exactly the phase the clock implies, with a single catch-up ding rather than a backlog. **Pause** freezes on sleep and waits for you.
- **Hover readout.** Hovering the menu bar item shows the phase and the exact time left — `Rest 03:12` — in a panel at roughly twice tooltip size, tinted to match the menu bar.
- **Analytics.** Sessions, cycles and reps completed and total work time for Today, the last 3 days, and the last 5 days, as calendar days in your timezone. A rep is a Work interval that ran to its end; one cut short by Stop or Skip adds its time but not a rep. Hover the panel to refresh it; the numbers are visible either way.
- **Focus tracking.** During Work the app reads your local [ActivityWatch](https://activitywatch.net/) server every 5 seconds and works out what the pomodoro is actually going on. Obsidian notes, reported with their full path by [link-activitywatcher](https://github.com/jsglazer/link-activitywatcher), are filed under a project at two levels — an area (a class, `Classes/*/*` by default) and a task inside it (an assignment folder `Classes/*/*/Assign/*`, otherwise the next folder down, such as `Inventory`) — by folder rules you can edit. On-task apps (Anki, Zotero, PDF readers), any PDF, and allowed sites (`library.gmu.edu`, `schar.gmu.edu` and `windows.cloud.microsoft` — GMU's Mason Labs virtual desktop — by default; existing settings gain the Mason Labs site through a one-time migration that syncs to your other Macs) count toward the project; every other app or site is a distraction; away time, the lock screen and the app itself are ignored. Documents reported by the Document sensor (below) go through the same folder rules, so a PDF, Word file or spreadsheet from `…/Classes/2026-B/Data - POGO 511/Chapters/` counts toward that class and task; a document outside every rule keeps the old rule (on-task app or any PDF counts toward the anchor). Mac only.
- **Document sensor.** Every 15 seconds (5–120, configurable), if the app in front is on the watched list, TechPomodoro records which documents of that app's file types it has open — full paths, read through `libproc` (the data `lsof` shows) from the app and its child processes, with no permission prompt — and sends them as an ActivityWatch heartbeat to the bucket `aw-watcher-documents_<hostname>` (type `app.documents.open`; data `app`, `bundle_id`, `count`, `paths`). It runs from launch to quit whether or not the timer is running, and never looks at Work or Rest. Default list: PDF Expert, Preview and Skim (`pdf`), Microsoft Word (`doc`, `docx`), Microsoft Excel (`xls`, `xlsx`, `csv`), Pages (`pages`), Numbers (`numbers`) and RStudio (`R`, `Rmd`, `qmd`, `Rproj`, `csv` — experimental; its documents live in the `rsession` child process). Edit the list, the extensions and the interval in the Focus tab, which also shows a live status line. The list and interval sync through iCloud; the on/off switch is per Mac. Only an aw-server on this Mac is ever written to; paths stay in this Mac's ActivityWatch database, which aw-sync copies to your other Macs. Package documents (`.pages`, `.numbers`) are reported as the package. The [activity-watch](https://github.com/jsglazer/activity-watch) dashboards use this bucket to credit document time to classes.
- **Drift alerts.** The anchor — the project a pomodoro is about — is inferred from the first 5 minutes, or pinned from the popover. While you are off it the countdown turns the drift colour (purple by default), and after 30 seconds straight off it one alert fires — ding, flash and a dialog naming what you are on — then not again until you are back on task. Drift can be judged per task or per class, and every threshold is configurable in the Focus tab. Time while the timer is paused is neutral: it counts neither on nor off task and never extends a drift, so resuming after a pause spent in another app does not alert, and there is no drift alert or drift colour while paused.
- **Focus history.** Each Work interval keeps its seconds per task, focus share, project switches and drift alerts. The Timer tab shows a live Focus panel (anchor, what you are on now, this interval's focus share) and today's time by project; the end-of-Work dialog adds a line like `Asgn03-TheoryHypotheses · 78% · 3 switches`.
- **Paper time.** Tag Work as time spent on paper — reading, writing, practising or watching away from the screen — and credit it to a class. See *Paper time* below.
- **Export.** The full interval history as CSV or JSON, focus and paper columns included.
- **Mirror with iPhone.** A running Mac timer shows on the iPhone's Lock Screen and Dynamic Island, even when the iPhone app isn't running (see *Mac timer on the iPhone* below). The other way round, while this Mac's own timer is idle, the iPhone's running timer shows in the menu bar and the popover header (`On <iPhone> · display only`), display only. The hover names the iPhone, and this Mac's ding, flash and popup go off at the iPhone's phase changes. The Mac checks iCloud for the iPhone's timer every 15 seconds and on wake or when the popover opens, then runs it forward itself, so its phase changes land on time even while the iPhone app is suspended. Starting the Mac's own timer takes over its menu bar.
- **Launch at login**, via `SMAppService`.
- **iCloud sync.** Settings and the interval history sync across your Macs, iPhone and iPad through your private iCloud database (CloudKit). History merges record by record, so nothing is ever overwritten. For settings, the most recent edit wins. Per-device choices stay on each device: the sound, launch at login, the sync switch itself, the ActivityWatch on/off and server URL, the paper tag, and **Publish paper time** with its file path. Each device has a **Sync via iCloud** switch with a live status line. Changes go up within seconds. Other devices' changes are pulled at launch, on wake, when the popover opens or the iOS app comes forward, and every 10 minutes.

## Paper time

Paper time replaces the iPhone *Paper Timer* Shortcut and the Obsidian Timer buttons: one timer, with pause, and no writes from the phone.

- **The tag.** In the popover's Timer tab (Mac) or the Timer screen (iPhone), the **Paper** control picks a class, a kind (Read, Write, Practice or Watch; choosing a class picks Read unless a kind was already chosen) and an optional note, with **Clear** beside it. It works idle (it applies from the next Start), running or paused. The tag is sticky. It stays through rests, long breaks, session rests, pause and resume, sleep and wake, and a relaunch, and only **Clear** or **Stop** removes it. A long reading block is just ordinary pomodoros, with the rests not counted. The tag belongs to the device and is never synced.
- **What gets logged.** Each tagged Work record carries `paper`: the class, kind, note and the exact wall-clock stretches it ran (a pause ends one stretch and the resume starts the next), so the stretches add up to the record's worked time. Rest phases never carry it. Changing the class or kind, or setting or clearing the tag, mid-Work closes the stretch so far as its own record and carries on as a new one. Only the record that reaches the end of the phase counts as a rep, so reps, cycles, sessions and total work time are exactly what they would have been without the change. Editing only the note doesn't split. Records written before paper time, and older app versions, still decode. Paper records are kept for 400 days instead of 30, so a whole term stays in the history.
- **The overlap rule: a tagged span counts once, for the tagged class.** While tagged, the focus anchor is pinned to the tag's class, matching the class name against the last folder of the focus area (`Classes/2026-B/Data - POGO 511` ↔ `Data - POGO 511`). Typing in that class's notes is on task, another class's notes drift as usual, and away time stays neutral, so reading on paper never raises a drift alert. The record's focus summary adds `paperOverlapSeconds`, the on-screen seconds credited to the tagged class. This is information only and is never added to paper time.
- **Display.** Tagged Work shows `P` in place of `W` in the menu bar (`24P`, or `P` beside the clock icon). The hover reads `Paper · Data - POGO 511 · Read 12:03`, and the popover header and the iPhone Timer screen show the tag. The Live Activity, both the iPhone's own and a mirrored Mac timer, names the class and kind, and so does the Mac's display of the iPhone's timer. The end-of-Work dialog adds a line like `Paper · Data · Read · 25 min`, and the analytics panel adds a **Paper today** line of minutes by class.
- **Two devices.** Setting a tag while the other device's timer is running (the iPhone's on the Mac, a Mac's on the iPhone) asks first, `A timer is running on <device> — tag this one anyway?`, so one sitting isn't logged twice.
- **Classes.** A synced list in Settings on both platforms: add, rename, delete and reorder. It starts as `Data - POGO 511`, `Econ - PUBP 720` and `RD - POGO 801`, and existing settings gain these through a one-time migration. Each name must match its class folder exactly, because the vault totals match on it.
- **Publishing (Mac, one Mac only).** **Publish paper time** in the Focus tab is a per-Mac switch, off by default; turn it on on one Mac only, because aw-sync copies ActivityWatch buckets between Macs. With it on, that Mac keeps the bucket `aw-watcher-paper_<hostname>` (event type `app.paper.sitting`) on its local aw-server. It holds one event per stretch of every paper sitting in the synced history, with `class`, `kind`, `note` and `record_id`. The Mac also rewrites one app-owned JSON file, by default `/Users/josh/VaultSchar/Classes/2026-B/Paper Log.json` (choose another with **Choose…**): `{ "version": 1, "generated", "sittings": [ { "id", "date", "class", "kind", "start", "end", "minutes", "note", "spans" } ] }`, sorted by start, with local dates and times. Both are rebuilt from the whole history whenever paper history changes, at most once a minute, and once at launch. The file is written whole and atomically (temporary file, then rename), never appended to, and nothing else in the vault is touched. The Focus tab shows the last write time, the number of sittings written and the last error.
- **Corrections.** History merges by id and is never overwritten, so an edit or a deletion is a correction record of its own (`PaperCorrection`), applied when the history is read. Corrections for one sitting apply oldest first, and the later edit of each field wins. A deletion is final, so a concurrent or later edit never brings a sitting back. A correction made before its sitting has synced waits for it. Corrections travel in the existing CloudKit zone as `IntervalRecord`-type records named `pc-<uuid>` carrying their JSON, so the CloudKit schema is unchanged; an older app version can't decode them as intervals and skips them.
- **Past sittings and the sittings list.** **Log a past sitting** (the Mac's Paper tab, or **+** in Analytics → Paper sittings on the iPhone) takes a class, a kind, a start, an end and a note. The end is a time on the start's day, and an end earlier than the start means past midnight. It adds a Work record with `paper`, `completed: false`, no focus summary and `manual: true`, so it adds paper time but never reps, cycles or work time. The list shows the last 14 days of sittings. You can edit the class, kind and note of any sitting, and the times of a manual one, or delete a sitting after a confirmation.

## iPhone and iPad

The same engine runs as a universal iOS app. It shares the schedule, thresholds, colours and analytics with the Mac app, and has its own shell built for a device that suspends and kills apps in the background:

- **Timer screen.** A large countdown inside a progress ring, coloured by the same threshold, rest-phase and red-when-paused rules as the menu bar. Tap the numbers to pause or resume, with Start/Stop (Stop asks first), Pause and Skip beneath them, and an *Up next* list of the coming boundaries with their times. On an iPad in landscape the analytics sit alongside.
- **Alerts with the app closed.** When a timer starts or resumes, every upcoming boundary is scheduled as a time-sensitive local notification, so alerts arrive on time with the app suspended or killed and without any background execution. The plan is rebuilt on pause, skip, stop or a settings change, and each time the app opens. It covers at most 60 boundaries or 24 hours, which keeps it under the iOS limit of 64 pending notifications even when the session repeats. With the app open, its own ding plays and the notification is suppressed, so each boundary sounds once.
- **Survives being killed.** A running session is saved as it changes and restored on launch, then fast-forwarded through any phases that ended while the app was gone. Those phases are logged to the history.
- **Lock screen and Dynamic Island.** A Live Activity shows the live countdown (red while paused), the progress bar and the next boundaries, with Pause/Resume, Skip and Stop buttons; Stop here is one tap, with no confirmation. The system renders the countdown by itself. A suspended app can't update its activity, so at a phase end two things move it on. First, the activity carries the next phase in full and goes stale exactly when the countdown runs out, so the system redraws it and it counts the next phase down by itself, with no app or Mac involved. Second, while a Mac with mirroring set up is awake, it pushes every phase change of the iPhone's timer to the activity, over a broadcast channel that Mac makes for this iPhone. With no Mac awake, the activity moves on by itself only once. After the following phase end it says what the schedule implies (`Now … — open to refresh`), and opening the app or tapping a button brings it back in step.
- **Mac timer on the iPhone.** While a Mac runs a timer, the iPhone's Live Activity and its Timer tab show that Mac's phase, countdown, progress and upcoming boundaries, labelled with the Mac's name. The iPhone app doesn't need to be running. The Mac pushes each change (start, pause, resume, skip, phase boundary, stop) straight to Apple's push service, which starts the activity by push and updates it through a broadcast channel. Nothing is pushed per second, because the phone runs the countdown itself. The mirror is display only: there are no buttons on the activity and no controls in the Timer tab while it shows a Mac timer. An active Mac timer takes precedence over the iPhone's own, which keeps running underneath. If several Macs are running, the one that changed most recently wins, and the iPhone goes back to its own timer when no Mac is active. If you swipe the mirrored activity away, the Mac's next change brings it back, or the app restarts it as soon as you open it. Each device has its own switch, and it covers both directions: **Mirror timer with iPhone** on the Mac and **Mirror timer with Mac** on the iPhone. With it on, the iPhone also publishes its own timer for the Macs (see *Mirror with iPhone* above). The Mac needs your APNs auth key (.p8), imported once in its Settings tab and kept in the login Keychain, never in the repo. Setup and a QA checklist are in [`MIRRORING.md`](MIRRORING.md). On macOS 26 the Mac also relays iPhone Live Activities to its own menu bar, and apps can't opt out. To hide it, turn off System Settings → Notifications → Notifications from iPhone → **Allow Live Activities from iPhone** (this applies to every app).
- **Sounds and haptics.** iOS has no system sound folder, so the app bundles five original tones (Glass, Chime, Bell, Tick, Pulse) generated by `Scripts/make-sounds.swift`. Glass is the default on both platforms. The Mac's menu bar flash becomes a haptic pulse, and the optional alert dialog becomes an in-app alert that closes itself on the same timer.
- **Export** through the share sheet.

Mac-only settings (menu bar mode, filled background, sleep behaviour, launch at login) are hidden on iOS and left untouched in the stored settings. Each device runs its own timer, and a Mac's timer can be mirrored onto the iPhone for display. Settings and history sync between devices through iCloud (see *iCloud sync* above, and the switch in Settings → iCloud).

## Install

The Mac app requires macOS 14 or later; the iOS app requires iOS 18 or later.

```sh
git clone https://github.com/jsglazer/tech-pomodoro.git
cd tech-pomodoro
xcodegen generate
xcodebuild -project TechPomodoro.xcodeproj -scheme TechPomodoro -configuration Release build
```

Or download the notarized DMG from [Releases](https://github.com/jsglazer/tech-pomodoro/releases) and drag the app into `/Applications`. To build a signed DMG yourself, run `Scripts/make-dmg.sh`.

Then copy the built `TechPomodoro.app` into `/Applications`. Launch at login only works from a properly registered bundle, so run it from `/Applications` rather than from the build directory.

For the iOS app, open `TechPomodoro.xcodeproj`, choose the `TechPomodoroiOS` scheme, select your development team, and run it on a device or simulator. Signing registers the Time Sensitive Notifications, iCloud and Push Notifications capabilities automatically. The iOS app isn't distributed through the App Store or TestFlight yet.

iCloud sync needs a one-time CloudKit setup: register the container, import `CloudKit/schema.ckdb` and deploy it to Production. See [`CloudKit/README.md`](CloudKit/README.md). Builds without the iCloud entitlement (such as `swift build` or an ad-hoc DMG) run normally and show sync as unavailable. Mirroring to the iPhone additionally needs an APNs auth key and the Broadcast capability on the iOS bundle ID; see [`MIRRORING.md`](MIRRORING.md).

## Architecture

The timer engine is a pure Swift state machine with no AppKit or SwiftUI anywhere in it:

- `Sources/TechPomodoroCore` — the reducer (which carries the paper tag and splits a Work record when it changes), paper history, corrections and the publishing formats (`Paper.swift`, `PaperPublishing.swift`), the analytics aggregator, the menu bar formatting model, the stores, and the focus tracker (`Focus.swift`), which takes ActivityWatch spans in and returns per-project attribution, the anchor and the drift streak. No UI imports, no reads of the system clock, no hardcoded paths. Every instant arrives as a parameter and every dependency is injected.
- `Sources/TechPomodoroApp` — the macOS shell: the status item, the SwiftUI popover, `NSSound`, `SMAppService`, the `NSWorkspace` sleep/wake observers, `ActivityWatchSource`, which reads aw-server, `DocumentSensorService`, which reads the front app's open files through `libproc` and writes the documents bucket on its own timer (the decisions — filtering, package paths, the process tree, the payload, backoff — are `DocumentSensor.swift` in the core), and `APNsClient`/`MirrorPusher`, which sign with the .p8 key (ES256 JWT) and push the mirror to APNs over HTTP/2, plus `PhoneTimerWatcher`, which reads the iPhone's timer from CloudKit, runs it forward for the menu bar, and pushes its phase changes to the iPhone's own Live Activity. One 1-second runloop timer drives display refresh only; it never advances the timer, which is always derived from an absolute end timestamp against the injected clock. Because remaining time is derived rather than accumulated, that timer carries a 0.25s tolerance so macOS can coalesce its wakeup instead of forcing the CPU out of idle every second.

- `Sources/TechPomodoroSync` — iCloud sync, shared by both apps: a `CKSyncEngine` over one zone in the private database, holding one record per history entry and one settings record, each carrying its value as JSON. It only moves records. Every merge rule (history union by id, last-writer-wins settings, device-only fields) is `SyncMerge` in the core, and is tested headlessly. Mirroring uses the same zone: `MirrorCloudStore` reads and writes `MirrorRecord`s (the iPhones' push-to-start tokens, each Mac's timer and the broadcast channel, plus each iPhone's own timer and its own channel). Which timer wins on either device, what to push and the APNs payloads are decided in the core's `Mirror.swift` and tested the same way.
- `Sources/TechPomodoroiOS` — the iOS shell: the SwiftUI tabs, the session snapshot, the notification scheduler, the bundled-tone player the Live Activity controller and `MirrorReceiver`, which publishes the push-to-start token and the iPhone's own timer, and watches the mirrored activity. The 1-second ticker runs only in the foreground, and background correctness comes entirely from `phaseEndsAt` plus the pre-scheduled notifications.
- `Sources/TechPomodoroLiveActivity` — the WidgetKit extension that draws the Live Activity. It never links the core; the app flattens the state into plain values in `Sources/TechPomodoroShared`, which both targets compile along with the Live Activity's App Intents.

`project.yml` (XcodeGen) defines all three targets and a single shared `MARKETING_VERSION`, so the Mac and iOS apps version together.

The popover and its `NSHostingController` are built on each open and torn down in `popoverDidClose` — deliberately, not incidentally. A retained hosting controller keeps its SwiftUI view graph and the popover's window alive after the popover is dismissed, and AppKit's display cycle will go on laying out that invisible view indefinitely; on 1.0.8 that cost 47–59% CPU with nothing on screen. Keep the rule: no SwiftUI view graph should outlive the popover that shows it.

That split is what makes the interesting parts testable: `swift test` covers the multi-tier transitions, sleep/wake fast-forward and resume-on-wake, the sync merge rules, the mirror rules (payloads, which timer wins, push only on change), pause/resume/stop/skip edge cases, threshold and paused colours, the phase letter, rolling analytics windows and rep counts, pruning, the persistence and export roundtrips, `BoundaryPlanner`, which lays out the upcoming boundaries the iOS app schedules as notifications, the focus rules, classification, anchor and drift logic, and aw-server wire format, the document sensor (path filtering, process tree, payload determinism, backoff, independence from the timer, and documents mapped through the focus rules), and paper time (tag stickiness through every phase, pause and sleep path, the split on a tag change with unchanged analytics, spans, the pinned anchor and overlap seconds, decoding with and without `paper`, the ActivityWatch events and vault file, and correction merging).

```sh
swift test
```
