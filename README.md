# tech-pomodoro

A menu-bar-only Pomodoro timer for macOS, with a companion iPhone and iPad app, built around a multi-tier schedule: work/rest reps build a cycle, cycles build a session, and each level gets its own break.

## Why

Most Pomodoro apps stop at "work, rest, long break". tech-pomodoro adds the level above that — a session of cycles with its own long rest at the end, optionally repeating forever — and puts the countdown itself in the menu bar, colour-shifting as work time runs out.

## Features

- **Multi-tier schedule.** Work and rest alternate for the reps of a cycle; the cycle closes with a long break; a session of cycles closes with a session rest. Every duration and count is configurable — type a number directly or use the stepper — and the session can repeat indefinitely.
- **The countdown is the control.** Click the numbers in the popover to pause or resume; Start (which becomes Stop once running), Pause and Skip sit beneath them. Skip ends the current phase at once and starts the next one running, even from a pause; the reps and cycles advance as if the phase had finished, a skipped Work interval is logged as the time actually worked, and no alert fires.
- **Menu bar as a countdown.** Show minutes remaining or a clock icon, optionally on a filled background. During Work the minutes turn yellow at a warning threshold and red at an alert threshold — both configurable by typing or stepping, either one disabled by setting it to 0. The rest phases get their own colour (green by default), and the ordinary countdown takes a colour of your choosing. When the timer is idle, the clock icon shows in cyan so the app stays easy to spot.
- **Alerts.** A system-sound ding and a menu bar flash at every interval, cycle, and session boundary — each independently switchable, and each repeatable up to ten times. Sounds are read from this Mac's sound folders, so the picker never offers something that will not play. An optional modal dialog can also demand a click at every boundary, for when a sound or a flash is easy to miss.
- **Sleep-aware.** By default the timer keeps counting against the wall clock through a system sleep and fast-forwards on wake to exactly the phase the clock implies, with a single catch-up ding rather than a backlog. It can pause on sleep instead.
- **Hover readout.** Hovering the menu bar item shows the phase and the exact time left — `Rest 03:12` — in a panel at roughly twice tooltip size, tinted to match the menu bar.
- **Analytics.** Sessions and cycles completed and total work time for Today, the last 3 days, and the last 5 days, as calendar days in your timezone. Hover the panel to refresh it; the numbers are visible either way.
- **Export.** The full interval history as CSV or JSON.
- **Launch at login**, via `SMAppService`.

## iPhone and iPad

The same engine runs as a universal iOS app. It shares the schedule, thresholds, colours and analytics with the Mac app, and has its own shell built for a device that suspends and kills apps in the background:

- **Timer screen.** A large countdown inside a progress ring, coloured by the same threshold and rest-phase rules as the menu bar. Tap the numbers to pause or resume, with Start/Stop, Pause and Skip beneath them, and an *Up next* list of the coming boundaries with their times. On an iPad in landscape the analytics sit alongside.
- **Alerts with the app closed.** When a timer starts or resumes, every upcoming boundary is scheduled as a time-sensitive local notification, so alerts arrive on time with the app suspended or killed and without any background execution. The plan is rebuilt on pause, skip, stop or a settings change, and each time the app opens. It covers at most 60 boundaries or 24 hours, which keeps it under the iOS limit of 64 pending notifications even when the session repeats. With the app open, its own ding plays and the notification is suppressed, so each boundary sounds once.
- **Survives being killed.** A running session is saved as it changes and restored on launch, then fast-forwarded through any phases that ended while the app was gone. Those phases are logged to the history.
- **Lock screen and Dynamic Island.** A Live Activity shows the live countdown, the progress bar and the next boundaries, with Pause/Resume, Skip and Stop buttons. The system renders the countdown by itself, but the activity can't move itself on to the next phase while the app is suspended. When the current phase runs out it marks itself stale and says what the schedule implies, and opening the app or tapping a button brings it back in step.
- **Sounds and haptics.** iOS has no system sound folder, so the app bundles five original tones (Glass, Chime, Bell, Tick, Pulse) generated by `Scripts/make-sounds.swift`. Glass is the default on both platforms. The Mac's menu bar flash becomes a haptic pulse, and the optional alert dialog becomes an in-app alert.
- **Export** through the share sheet.

Mac-only settings (menu bar mode, filled background, sleep behaviour, launch at login) are hidden on iOS and left untouched in the stored settings. Each device keeps its own timer and history; syncing the two between devices is not built yet.

## Install

The Mac app requires macOS 14 or later; the iOS app requires iOS 17 or later.

```sh
git clone https://github.com/jsglazer/tech-pomodoro.git
cd tech-pomodoro
xcodegen generate
xcodebuild -project TechPomodoro.xcodeproj -scheme TechPomodoro -configuration Release build
```

Or download the notarized DMG from [Releases](https://github.com/jsglazer/tech-pomodoro/releases) and drag the app into `/Applications`. To build a signed DMG yourself, run `Scripts/make-dmg.sh`.

Then copy the built `TechPomodoro.app` into `/Applications`. Launch at login only works from a properly registered bundle, so run it from `/Applications` rather than from the build directory.

For the iOS app, open `TechPomodoro.xcodeproj`, choose the `TechPomodoroiOS` scheme, select your development team, and run it on a device or simulator. Signing registers the Time Sensitive Notifications capability automatically. The iOS app isn't distributed through the App Store or TestFlight yet.

## Architecture

The timer engine is a pure Swift state machine with no AppKit or SwiftUI anywhere in it:

- `Sources/TechPomodoroCore` — the reducer, the analytics aggregator, the menu bar formatting model, and the stores. No UI imports, no reads of the system clock, no hardcoded paths. Every instant arrives as a parameter and every dependency is injected.
- `Sources/TechPomodoroApp` — the macOS shell: the status item, the SwiftUI popover, `NSSound`, `SMAppService`, and the `NSWorkspace` sleep/wake observers. One 1-second runloop timer drives display refresh only; it never advances the timer, which is always derived from an absolute end timestamp against the injected clock. Because remaining time is derived rather than accumulated, that timer carries a 0.25s tolerance so macOS can coalesce its wakeup instead of forcing the CPU out of idle every second.

- `Sources/TechPomodoroiOS` — the iOS shell: the SwiftUI tabs, the session snapshot, the notification scheduler, the bundled-tone player and the Live Activity controller. The 1-second ticker runs only in the foreground, and background correctness comes entirely from `phaseEndsAt` plus the pre-scheduled notifications.
- `Sources/TechPomodoroLiveActivity` — the WidgetKit extension that draws the Live Activity. It never links the core; the app flattens the state into plain values in `Sources/TechPomodoroShared`, which both targets compile along with the Live Activity's App Intents.

`project.yml` (XcodeGen) defines all three targets and a single shared `MARKETING_VERSION`, so the Mac and iOS apps version together.

The popover and its `NSHostingController` are built on each open and torn down in `popoverDidClose` — deliberately, not incidentally. A retained hosting controller keeps its SwiftUI view graph and the popover's window alive after the popover is dismissed, and AppKit's display cycle will go on laying out that invisible view indefinitely; on 1.0.8 that cost 47–59% CPU with nothing on screen. Keep the rule: no SwiftUI view graph should outlive the popover that shows it.

That split is what makes the interesting parts testable: `swift test` covers the multi-tier transitions, sleep/wake fast-forward, pause/resume/stop/skip edge cases, threshold colours, rolling analytics windows, pruning, the persistence and export roundtrips, and `BoundaryPlanner`, which lays out the upcoming boundaries the iOS app schedules as notifications.

```sh
swift test
```
